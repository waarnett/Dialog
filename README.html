Dialog

A moderated group conversation between you and several AI models (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, or any other model OpenRouter offers, and models running in LM Studio on your own computer), running as a single web page in your browser; Safari and Chrome are both known to work. You decide who speaks next; every participant sees the same conversation, except for private exchanges you have with one of them.

Getting started

  1. Get an OpenRouter key. At openrouter.ai, sign in, add a small amount of credit (Credits page), then create a key (Keys page). Give the key a credit limit: it caps what Dialog can ever spend. A limit is a cap, not money; OpenRouter never spends past the credit you've bought. A limit can also be set to reset daily, weekly or monthly.
  2. Open index.html in your browser (double-click it, or drag it onto a browser window). Keep README.html, this guide, in the same folder: the page links to it. (Before 2026 Oct 11 the page was called dialog.html. In Chrome, the new name opens with your settings and conversation as they were; to be safe in any browser, first use Save settings to a file… in the old page, ticking Include my keys, and restore that file in the new one if anything is missing.)
  3. Click Settings: paste the key, put in your name (it starts as "John Doe") and, if you like, a few sentences about you. Click Done.
  4. Add participants. Under Participants, on the right, press + Add a seat and choose a model. Each new seat is named after its model ("Claude", "Grok"…) and takes that family's color; rename it if you like. Add as many as you want, including several of the same model.
  5. Optional: a management key, to see your remaining credit. OpenRouter shows the account's remaining credit only to a management key, a separate kind of key that can create, change and delete your API keys but can't talk to the models. Create one at openrouter.ai/settings/management-keys, giving it an expiration date (90 days or a year), and paste it into Settings under "OpenRouter management key". Dialog uses it for nothing but reading your credit. The risk: someone who got hold of it could make new keys without limits, so the most they could spend is your remaining credit (plus any automatic top-up, if you've turned that on).

Until there is a first entry, the conversation area shows a short welcome: how to begin, including a reminder that the house rules can be edited in Settings, beside an engraving of Socrates. Steps you've already done (the key, adding seats) drop off. Under the steps, a link opens this README in a new tab, and a note points out that the participants don't get the hidden instructions their makers' own chat apps use (see How the participants are kept separate, below), so they may answer differently than those apps would.

The top bar

  • This conversation: what its replies have cost, as reported by OpenRouter. OpenRouter's 5.5% fee on credit purchases comes on top.
  • Credit left (only with a management key): your account's remaining credit, across all your keys. Hover for credit bought and used; click it to open OpenRouter's Credits page in a new tab, where you can add more.
  • This month: what this API key has spent in the current calendar month. Months begin at midnight UTC, which is 5 pm Pacific in summer and 4 pm in winter. Hover for the key's all-time spending and its limit.
  • Save All (or Save Selected), New conversation, Settings: see below.

A notice appears under the top bar when something needs attention: no API key yet (click the notice to open Settings), or the browser refusing to let Dialog save, in which case the conversation and settings will be lost when the page closes.

Participants

Each participant ("seat") has a card on the right:

  • ⠿ (the handle), left of the name, moves the card: drag it up or down, and a line shows where it will land. Or click it and press ↑ or ↓. The order is the order of the To menu, of the participant list every seat is given, and of Participants in saved files. Since that list is part of every seat's instructions, a new order means each seat's next turn re-reads the conversation at full price once (no re-reading discount). Sleeping participants' folded cards stay at the end and have no handle.
  • Name, at the top of the card, in the seat's color. Click it to rename. The name is what the others see ("Skeptic says: …") and what appears in the margin of the conversation. The logo of the model's maker comes just after the name (see The logos, below).
  • + copy adds another seat with the same model and settings, a new name ("Claude 2") and a darker shade of the color, without the role.
  • × removes the seat. Its earlier contributions stay in the conversation.
  • The model button shows the model's name and opens the model picker, a list of every OpenRouter model with its price (dollars per million tokens, input / output), after LM Studio's models if you use LM Studio (see Models on this computer):
    • Type a few words ("opus", "gemini pro") to narrow the list; the current model is marked "(current)".
    • Sort, beside the search box, puts the list in order by name, or by input or output price, lowest or highest first. Models at the same price are in name order. Routers, which pass each request to a model they choose and so have no fixed price, are listed with the price "varies" and come last either way. Dialog remembers the order you chose.
    • Click a model, or use ↑ ↓ (Page Up / Page Down to jump) and Return.
    • Escape, Cancel, or a click outside the list closes it without changing anything.
    • Any model id can be typed in full (it contains a "/"); a "Use … as typed" row appears.
    • The list comes from OpenRouter. Dialog keeps a copy and gets a new one when its copy is more than 12 hours old: when the page opens, and when you open the picker (even if the page has been open all along). The line under the list says how old the copy is ("from OpenRouter's list as of 15:28 today").
    • Refresh list, beside Cancel, gets a new list at once: use it for a model that has only just appeared. While the new list is on its way the button reads "Refreshing…" and the old list stays; your search and the highlighted model stay too. If the new list can't be fetched, the line under the list says why, and the old list stays.
    • Dialog always asks OpenRouter itself for the list, never a copy the browser kept from an earlier visit.
  • Under the button: the model's id and price, or a warning if the id isn't in OpenRouter's current list. For an id that always follows a family's newest model (it begins with "~", such as "~deepseek/deepseek-flash-latest"), the id shown is that of the model it stands for now, such as "deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017"; the "~" name is on the model button above it. On a Mac, pointing at the id shows the "~" id too.
  • The logos of the models' makers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and so on) sit on each card's top line, just after the seat's name, folded cards included. They are the ones OpenRouter shows beside each model on its own pages, loaded from the same places: OpenRouter's site, or for some makers (xAI, Z.ai, Xiaomi, NVIDIA, Amazon, MiniMax, StepFun) Google's service for a website's icon. Dialog knows the logos of about twenty makers; for any other, or if a logo doesn't load, the card shows the maker's initial in a small square instead.
  • Web search and page reading are always on; there is nothing to set (see Searching and reading web pages, below). If the model can't use tools, the card says "Can't use tools, so no web search or page reading".
  • Thinking offers exactly the levels the chosen model lists on its OpenRouter page, highest first, with the model's own default marked. Models with no thinking setting show "not adjustable". Hidden thinking is billed as output.
  • Role opens a box for that participant's standing instructions, e.g. "argue the skeptic's side". It shows "(set)" or "(none)".
  • Sleep, beside Thinking, benches the participant for a while without removing it. Its card folds up to just its name, its maker's logo and the Sleep box, and moves to the end of the list. While asleep it sits out "team" rounds and isn't the one Return asks when your message names no one. It still replies if you name it at the end of your message, and stays in the To menu for private messages. It keeps its place in the order: untick Sleep and the card unfolds where it was. (If you move other cards meanwhile, it comes back among the same neighbors.) Sleeping participants still appear in the participant list every seat is given, since they may be asked again.
  • Speak asks the participant to reply now. Only one participant speaks at a time, except in an independent round, when all of them do at once (see Talking, below). While a participant speaks, its card is outlined, the Speak buttons wait, and its Stop button ends its reply early.
  • The participant's cost so far in this conversation, at the bottom right of the card.

Talking

Words at the end of your message tell Dialog who should reply when you press Return. Dialog takes them off before anyone sees your message, and marks it with a stage direction instead, such as "(to Grok)". Each is explained in full below.

End your message with Example What happens
a name "…what do you think, Grok?" Grok replies.
several names "…, Gemini and Claude?" Each replies once, in that order, seeing the replies before it.
names and "independently" "…, Claude and Grok, independently" They reply at once, without seeing each other's replies.
"team" "…what do you think, team?" Every awake participant replies once, in turn.
"team" and "independently" "…team, independently" Every awake participant replies at once, without seeing the others' replies.
"privately" "…, Grok, privately" The same as without "privately", but private: only the participants named see your message and their replies. With no name, it goes to whoever spoke last.
"only" "…, Grok only" The same as "privately", but only right after a name, a list of names or "team".

"parallel" and "in parallel" mean the same as "independently", and can come before or after the names or "team". Without any of these endings, Return asks whoever spoke last. ⌘↩ never asks anyone: it just adds your message (privately, if it ends with "privately" or "only").

  • Your input box is at the bottom. With To: Everyone, Add to conversation, or ⌘↩ (Command-Return; Ctrl+Enter on other systems), puts your message into the conversation; nobody answers until you press a participant's Speak button. Shift-Return or Option-Return starts a new line in the box.

  • Return adds your message and asks a participant to reply, so a back-and-forth needs no clicking:

    • If your message ends with a participant's name, that participant replies: "…what do you think, Grok?" asks Grok. Case and punctuation don't matter, the name must be a word of its own ("Claudeish" doesn't count), and the longest name wins ("…Claude 2" asks Claude 2, not Claude). It goes by the last word only, so "I agree with Claude." asks Claude too.
    • A list of names at the end asks just those participants, once each, in the order you typed them: "…what do you think, Gemini and Claude?" has Gemini reply, then Claude (who sees Gemini's reply). Separate the names with spaces, commas, "and", "&", "+" or "/". Add "independently" (or "parallel", or "in parallel") before or after the list ("…, Claude and Grok, independently") and they all reply at once, each without seeing the others' replies from that turn (see "…independently, team", below). A name given twice counts once. Your message is marked "(to Gemini and Claude, in turn)".
    • Otherwise the participant who spoke last replies, unless it has been put to sleep (see Sleep, above); then the last awake one to speak does. Only the open conversation counts; a private exchange doesn't change who is next.
    • The name is for Dialog, not part of your message. Dialog takes it off ("What do you think, Grok?" becomes "What do you think?") and shows your message with a stage direction under your name: "(to Grok)". The participants are told the same thing in plain words ("Bill says (to Grok): …"), so everyone still knows whom you asked.

    The note beside the button shows who it will be and changes as you type: "↩ adds, then Grok replies · ⌘↩ just adds". If no participant has spoken yet and none is named, or while a reply is still arriving, Return only adds your message. With the box empty it does nothing, so a stray keypress never pays for a reply. Return that finishes typing a character with an input method (for Chinese or Japanese, say) is left alone.

  • "…, team" at the end of your message has every participant reply once, in turn, when you press Return. Like a name, "team" (and "independently") is for Dialog: it's taken off your message, which is marked "(to everyone, in turn)" or "(to everyone, independently)", and the participants are told that in plain words instead. They go around the table in card order, starting with the one after the last speaker, so nobody speaks twice in a row. Each sees the replies before it. The note beside the button says how many and who goes first ("↩ adds, then all 5 reply in turn, Claude first", or "the 3 awake" when some are asleep); hover over it for the whole order.

    • "…independently, team" (or "…team, independently") has each reply without seeing the others' replies from that round, which avoids everyone following whoever answered first. "parallel" or "in parallel" means the same ("…in parallel, team", "…team, parallel"). Those replies are marked "(answering independently)", here and in saved files. Afterwards everyone sees them all, as usual.
    • An independent round runs all at once. Since no participant waits for another's reply, every request goes out together. The replies take their places in the conversation straight away, in the round's order, each showing "thinking…" until its first words arrive, and then fill in side by side; nothing moves while you read. The round takes about as long as its slowest participant, not all of them one after another. Nobody goes first, so the note beside the button says "↩ adds, then all 5 reply independently, at once"; hover over it for the order the replies will appear in.
    • Because "in parallel" is ordinary English too, a question that ends "…can these run in parallel, team?" also starts an independent round, and is sent as "…can these run?". The note beside the button shows this before you press Return; reword the question if that isn't what you meant.
    • Typing slips are forgiven ("tema", "taem", "teamm", "independantly", "idnependently", "paralel", "paralell", "parralel"), but real words a slip or two away don't count: "…in the long term." doesn't start a round, nor do "tea", "steam", "teams", "tear", "beam" and the like, and "parallels", "parable" and "parallax" aren't "parallel". A participant's name at the end still wins: "…what about you, Grok?" asks Grok alone.
    • Sleeping participants sit rounds out, including one put to sleep while a round is running (if you'd named it in a list, it still replies). If everyone is asleep, "…, team" just adds your message, without "team", and nobody replies.
    • Participants without a model are skipped. While a round runs, the Speak buttons wait. In a round taken in turn, Stop ends the reply under way and the rest of the round; in an independent round, each card's Stop ends only that participant's reply, and the others go on. A failed reply is skipped and the round goes on. A round costs about as much as all the participants' replies together.

  • Speak also adds whatever you've typed and attached before the participant replies, so one press both says something and asks someone to answer. To have one participant respond to another, press the Speak button of the one who should respond, with the box empty.

  • To: Privately: <name> sends a message to that participant only. The box turns parchment-colored and the button reads "Send privately to <name>"; Return and ⌘↩ do the same. The participant answers at once. Both entries are set on parchment and marked like stage directions, "(privately, to Grok)" and "(privately, to John Doe)", and no other participant ever sees them. The participant itself remembers them, as it would in its own chat window.

  • "…, privately" as the very last word of your message does the same without the To menu: "What do you really think, Grok, privately?" goes to Grok alone, and Grok answers. Like a name, the word is for Dialog and comes off your message. While your message ends with it, the box turns parchment-colored, the button reads Add privately, and the note beside it says who will reply ("↩ sends privately; Grok replies · ⌘↩ just adds it privately").

    • Whom it goes to follows the usual rules for Return: the names at the end (before "privately"), or, if you name no one, the participant who spoke last. Press a card's Speak instead and, if you named no one, it goes to that participant.
    • Several names make a private group: "…, Claude and Grok, privately" goes to Claude and Grok, who reply in turn (or at once, with "independently"). Each sees your message and the other's reply; nobody else ever sees any of it. The entries are marked "(privately, to Claude and Grok, in turn)", "(privately, to Bill and Grok)" and so on. "…, team, privately" goes to every participant who is awake; sleeping ones never see it.
    • "only" means the same as "privately", but only right after a name, a list of names or "team": "…what do you really think, Grok only?" goes to Grok alone. Anywhere else it is just a word, so "Answer in one sentence only" is an ordinary message.
    • ⌘↩ or Add privately puts the message in privately without asking anyone to reply.
    • Nothing goes in for everyone by mistake. If no one has spoken yet and you name no one, the note says "Say whom it's for" and the button waits; while replies are still arriving, it says to wait until they've finished.
    • Typing slips are forgiven ("privatly", "privatley"), but "private" doesn't count, and neither does "privately" anywhere but at the very end: "We spoke privately, Grok" asks Grok openly.

  • Replies appear as they are written. "thinking…" shows until the first words arrive. Replies are formatted from Markdown (headings, lists, tables, code); links open in a new tab. If a model begins its reply with its own label ("Claude says:"), Dialog removes it.

  • Each turn shows, in the margin, a tick box for saving (see Saving, below), the time it began and the speaker's name. After the text come any sources, then the cost, the speed (below), and "stopped early" if you stopped it.

  • Tap (or click) a reply's cost and speed for the full particulars in their place: the exact model that answered (for a "~" id, the model it stood for then) and the thinking level it was given; the cost with its token counts, including how much was re-read at the discount and how much was stored for re-reading; and the speed's breakdown. Tap again for the short version. Particulars that are open when you save are saved in full too. Nothing needs hovering, so this works on an iPad too. (On a Mac, pointing at a participant's name also shows the model and thinking level.)

  • × on a turn (it appears when you hover over the turn) removes it, so no participant sees it from then on.

  • Stopped replies keep what arrived, and the others will see it.

  • When a reply fails, Dialog shows OpenRouter's message plus whatever particulars it sent: which host failed, the host's own error code or words, and the request id ("gen-…"). When a host fails partway through, OpenRouter replaces its message with a generic one (for example "JSON error injected into SSE stream"), so those particulars are often the only real clue; the request id can be looked up on OpenRouter's Activity page. Try again replaces the failed reply with a fresh attempt by the same participant, without touching anything you've typed. Failed replies are never shown to any participant.

  • When a model is withdrawn from OpenRouter, that seat's card shows "… is not in OpenRouter's current list" (once Dialog's copy of the list is refreshed: within 12 hours, or at once with Refresh list in the model picker), and pressing its Speak button gives a failed reply that says the model is no longer listed and to choose another with the seat's model button. Nothing else is affected. The seat keeps its name, role and earlier turns; its new model will take those earlier replies as its own. Model ids beginning with "~" (such as "~google/gemini-pro-latest") follow the newest version, so they don't go away. If a model is still listed but all its hosts are down, the failed reply says that may be the cause; try again later.

Searching and reading web pages

Every participant whose model can use tools may search the web and read web pages, deciding for itself when to. OpenRouter does both on its behalf.

  • Searching: Claude, and models that use OpenRouter's own search (DeepSeek and Qwen, for instance), stop at 10 searches per reply. Models that use their maker's own search (OpenAI's, Google's, xAI's) set their own pace; OpenRouter doesn't pass them a limit. A search costs about 1 to 3 cents. Sources a participant used are listed under its reply.
  • Reading a page: a participant can read any web page or online PDF whose address comes up in the conversation, whether you typed it or another participant did. Reading costs at most a tenth of a cent, plus the page's text at the model's input price: a long page of 30,000 tokens adds about 12 cents with Claude Opus 5.5, 30 cents with GPT-6 Astra.
  • Only the reader sees the page. The others see only what it says about the page, and Dialog keeps only the replies, not the pages. So if another participant wants the details, it reads the page for itself, and a participant that wants to look again later reads it again. Each reading is paid once. That is usually cheaper than attaching the page as a file, which every participant re-reads every time it speaks: the same page attached would cost about 29 cents over 10 turns of one Claude seat (re-reads are discounted), and $1.20 with a model that gives no discount. Attach it instead (save it as a PDF and drag it in) when everyone should work from exactly the same copy, or when a site won't let OpenRouter in.

Speed

After each reply, next to its cost, is its speed in tokens per second: the tokens the model wrote (its hidden thinking included, since that is billed and takes time) divided by the seconds from pressing Speak to the last word. That time includes thinking, any web searches and network delay, so it measures how fast the participant answers you, not raw model speed. Tap it for the breakdown: how long until the first words appeared, and how fast the visible text arrived after that, which is the closer measure of raw generation speed. Stopped and failed replies have no speed (OpenRouter reports no token count for them).

Attaching files

Drag files onto the conversation (a dashed outline shows where to drop) or click Attach…. They wait above your input box (× drops one) and go out with your next message: with Add to conversation, with a Speak press, or privately when To: names a participant. In the conversation they appear under the message: images as pictures, other files by name and size.

  • Text files (Markdown, plain text, CSV, code and the like) are sent as text.

  • Images (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF) are sent as images; large ones are scaled down first. A participant whose model can't view images is told an image was shared.

  • PDFs go to models that read PDFs themselves; for the others, OpenRouter converts them to text with its free converter. Models in LM Studio get no PDFs: they are told a PDF was shared, so attach the text instead (copy it into a text file) if they should read it (Dialog asks for that one explicitly, because the paid default costs $2 per 1,000 pages and a PDF is re-sent every turn).

  • Word, Pages, Excel and similar files can't be read; Dialog says so and suggests exporting them as PDF. Other refusals (a file too large, a type Dialog can't read) appear above the input box.

  • What some models take in, per OpenRouter's model list on 2026 Oct 07. Text files work with every model, since they arrive as ordinary text. Dialog checks OpenRouter's current list each time, so it adapts if this changes.

    Model Images PDFs
    Claude Opus 5.5 yes read by the model itself
    GPT-6 Astra yes read by the model itself
    Gemini Pro (latest; now Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) yes read by the model itself
    Grok 4.7 yes read by the model itself
    DeepSeek Pro (latest; now DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813) no; told an image was shared converted to text by OpenRouter
    Qwen 3.8 Max, Max Prime, Omni Flash yes converted to text by OpenRouter

    A model can misread an image even when it receives it. To check that one really saw a picture, ask about a detail only the picture shows.

  • Cost: like everything in the conversation, a file is re-read by each participant every time it speaks. Text files show their approximate size in tokens, and Dialog asks before attaching one over 50,000 tokens.

  • Files are stored in the browser separately from the conversation. Browsers allow each page only a few megabytes in all, so a very large file may not fit; it is then marked "not saved" and lasts until the page closes. Removing a turn, or starting a new conversation, frees the space its files used. A file that could not be kept is marked "no longer available", and participants are told so.

Files the participants make

A participant can hand you a file: a document, a table, a drawing, a small web page. Ask for one ("…put that in a Markdown file, Claude"), or a participant may offer one when a file is clearly the best way to deliver something. Dialog tells every participant how (see House rules, under Settings).

  • Kinds: Markdown (.md), plain text (.txt, or another text format such as .csv), drawings (.svg) and web pages (.html). A web page can carry its own styles and scripts: a calculator, a chart, a small game.

  • Each file is a card under the reply it came in: its name, kind and size, a small picture of a drawing, and three buttons.

    • Open shows it in a new tab: Markdown formatted, text as written, a drawing full size, a web page running. If nothing opens, allow pop-up windows for the page.
    • Save… puts it on your computer (see Where files go, under Saving). A web page goes through the pretty-printer first (see Laid out for reading, under Saving); other files are saved exactly as written.
    • × removes it. The card stays, struck out, and no participant sees the file from then on. Save it first if you want to keep it.

    While a reply is arriving, it says which files are done and which one is being written. If a reply ends, or you stop it, before a file is finished, the card says the file may be incomplete.

  • A web page opens walled off from Dialog. A page a model wrote could hold anything, including a script meant to read what Dialog keeps in your browser, your OpenRouter keys among it. So Open shows the page inside a sandboxed frame: the browser treats it as a separate site, with no way into Dialog's storage or the Dialog tab. Its scripts run, and it can load pictures or libraries from the web, but it can't reach Dialog. Drawings, Markdown and text open the same way, with no scripts allowed at all.

  • A saved web page has no such wall. In Chrome, every page opened from a file on your computer shares one storage, Dialog's included. So view a participant's web page with Open, and double-click a saved copy only if you've read it, or trust it as you would any page downloaded from the web.

  • The others see the files. A file goes to the other participants right after the text of its reply, in the same form as the files you attach, so anyone can comment on it or improve it. A file in a private reply stays private. To revise a file, a participant writes it again under the same name. The newer version then replaces the older one for everyone: the older card says "A later version replaces this one", and its contents are no longer sent.

  • Cost: like a file you attach, a participant's file is re-read by every participant each time it speaks, until it is removed or replaced. A 20 KB web page is about 5,000 tokens: about 2 cents a turn for Claude Opus 5.5 at full price, a tenth of a cent at the re-reading discount. Removing files nobody needs any more saves money.

  • Files are stored in the browser like attached files (see Attaching files), and the saved conversation includes them: drawings as pictures, and every file's text in a section you click to open. A saved conversation never runs a web page; it shows its text.

Saving and starting over

  • Save All (top bar) saves the whole conversation as one web page, styled like Dialog and readable in any browser: participants with their models, thinking levels and roles; the first sentence of your About-you text, under your name; every turn, set as a dialogue, with private exchanges included and marked (and the other stage directions too: "(to Grok)", "(to everyone, in turn)", "(answering independently)"); attached images shown, attached text files tucked into a section you click to open, PDFs listed by name; the participants' files (see above); sources; costs, with the conversation's total at the end (in full, as on screen, for any reply whose particulars you have open). A reply given with a different model or thinking level from the one its seat has now (you changed it since, or removed the seat) is marked with the one it used. Failed replies are left out. The file is named like "Dialog 2026 Oct 07 2205.html".
  • Save Selected: tick the box beside any turns (yours or the participants'; the box is faint until you point at the turn) and the top-bar button becomes Save Selected. It saves just those turns, in order, and lists under Participants only those who speak in them; you appear there only if one of your own turns is ticked. The heading says how many turns were chosen, and the total at the end is for those turns. The file is named like "Dialog 2026 Oct 07 2205 selected.html". Ticks stay after saving, so you can adjust and save again; untick them all and the button is Save All again. Ticks are not remembered when the page closes, and New conversation clears them.
  • First sentence of About you: in either kind of file, only the first sentence (or first line, if that ends sooner) is shown, and a note says it is only the first sentence. Full stops in abbreviations such as "e.g.", "Dr." or "Ph.D." don't end the sentence. The participants themselves still get the whole text.
  • Where files go. In Chrome (and Edge), Dialog opens the Mac's standard Save dialog, so you choose the folder and can change the name; Cancel saves nothing. Safari doesn't let a web page open that dialog. To get it in Safari anyway, set Safari Settings › General › File download location to "Ask for each download"; Safari then shows the same Save dialog for every download. Otherwise Safari saves to your Downloads folder, asking permission each time unless, in Safari Settings › Websites › Downloads, "When visiting other websites" is set to Allow. This applies to conversations and to settings files alike.
  • Laid out for reading. Every HTML file Dialog writes goes through a pretty-printer, a program that lays source text out for people to read (Dialog uses js-beautify): saved conversations, participants' web pages when you save them, and index.html and README.html themselves. Each element goes on its own line, indented two spaces, and long lines are broken at a space near 100 characters. Only the layout of the text changes: the page looks exactly the same in a browser, and text in preformatted blocks (such as the files in a saved conversation) is left as it was.
  • New conversation clears the conversation (save first). Participants and settings stay.
  • Between visits, Dialog remembers the conversation, the participants and your settings.

Models on this computer (LM Studio)

Alongside OpenRouter's models, a participant can use a model running in LM Studio on your own computer. It costs nothing per reply, and its messages never leave the computer. LM Studio participants and OpenRouter participants can share one conversation; a conversation with only LM Studio participants needs no OpenRouter key at all.

Setting up:

  1. In LM Studio, download a model and load it.
  2. Start LM Studio's server with CORS on. CORS is what lets a web page, such as Dialog, talk to the server. The surest way is in Terminal: ~/.lmstudio/bin/lms server start --cors LM Studio's own caution applies: while CORS is on, any website you visit can use the server (only from browsers on this computer). Stop the server when you're done (~/.lmstudio/bin/lms server stop) or start it again without --cors.
  3. In Dialog's Settings, click the LM Studio server box: it fills in the usual address, http://localhost:1234. If your server has another address, type it instead.
  4. Press a seat's model button. LM Studio's chat models come first in the list, marked "(LM Studio)", with "loaded" beside any model loaded now; the line under the list says how many there are, or why LM Studio couldn't be reached. Choose one, and a new seat is named after the model ("Gemma 4 31B QAT"). A model not loaded yet is loaded by LM Studio when it is first asked, which can take a while.

Which browser:

  • Chrome works with the online Dialog. The first time, Chrome asks whether the page may reach devices on your local network; allow it.
  • Safari doesn't let the online Dialog (an https page) reach a server on this computer. It works when Dialog is opened from a copy of index.html on your disk (keep README.html beside it): double-click the copy. Safari keeps that copy's settings and conversation apart from the online page's, so you set it up again there (Save settings to a file and Restore from a file carry your setup across).

What's different about an LM Studio participant:

  • No cost is shown: the reply's speed and token counts are, as for any reply.
  • No web search or page reading: those are done by OpenRouter.
  • No thinking setting: the card's Thinking menu is fixed at the model's own default. A model that thinks before answering shows "thinking…" until its first words appear; the thinking itself isn't shown.
  • Images go to models LM Studio lists as able to view them; others are told an image was shared. PDFs aren't sent (see Attaching files).
  • The conversation must fit the model's window. LM Studio loads a model with a fixed context length (often 8,192 tokens, though many models support far more). A conversation longer than that fails with a message saying how many tokens it needs; in LM Studio, load the model again with a larger context length, or start a new conversation.
  • If the card says the model is not among LM Studio's models, it was removed from LM Studio, or the id was typed wrongly. LM Studio answers such a request with whichever model it has loaded, so choose the model again.
  • If Dialog couldn't reach LM Studio, check that the server is running and was started with CORS on, and that the address in Settings is right.

Settings

  • OpenRouter API key, with Show to check what you pasted.

  • OpenRouter management key (optional), to show your remaining credit; see Getting started.

  • LM Studio server (optional): the address of LM Studio's server on this computer, usually http://localhost:1234, for using its models; see Models on this computer (LM Studio). Leave it empty if you don't use LM Studio.

  • Your name: what participants call you, and the name on your turns.

  • About you: a few sentences every participant receives, on every turn, after the house rules (and Dialog's note on making files) and before its role.

  • House rules: the instructions every participant receives. The defaults cover:

    • how the conversation works: who's present, how messages are labeled, not to speak for others, private exchanges;
    • how to contribute: agree briefly rather than repeat, keep contributions to a few paragraphs unless asked for more, no flattery of you or of each other, confidence as a percentage for claims that matter, with what would raise or lower it, and never 0% or 100% without hard evidence;
    • how to think: test ideas against particular cases, carry an idea into a new context only when the situations are clearly analogous, admit confusion, search or ask when information is missing, present the reasonable alternatives with evidence against your favorite, keep hypotheticals exact, keep facts apart from value judgements, show each step of a chain of reasoning, and prefer a qualified answer to a confident mistake.

    Placeholders filled in for each participant: {name} its name, {humanName} your name, {participants} everyone present with their models, {date} today's date. Edit freely; Restore default rules undoes your edits. A role comes after the house rules, so a role can override them, e.g. "for this role, argue the other side as strongly as you honestly can."

    After the house rules, Dialog adds a short note of its own that you can't edit: how to make a file (see Files the participants make). It is part of how Dialog works, like the way messages are labeled, so editing the rules can't break it.

    When the default rules change in a new version of Dialog, a copy you never edited is updated to match. An edited copy is left alone, except that a default sentence that has since been reworded is updated where it still appears word for word.

  • Save settings to a file… downloads your name, About you, the house rules and every participant (name, model, thinking level, role, color and whether it's asleep) as one small file, named like "Dialog settings 2026 Oct 09 1722.json". Use it as a backup, or to move Dialog to another browser or computer. Include my keys in the saved file adds your OpenRouter keys; it starts unticked each time you open Settings, because anyone who has a file with your keys could spend your credit.

  • Restore from a file… reads such a file, says what it holds and asks before replacing your name, About you, house rules and participants. Keys in the file replace yours; if it has none, yours stay. The conversation isn't changed: turns by a participant that the restored settings don't include stay, as when you remove a seat.

  • Done closes Settings; so do Escape and a click anywhere outside the Settings window. Changes are kept as you type.

How the participants are kept separate

Every request goes through your one OpenRouter key. OpenRouter passes it on to the company hosting the model using OpenRouter's own account there. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI and DeepSeek never see your key; to them, all of Dialog's requests look like ordinary OpenRouter traffic. (OpenRouter's optional "bring your own key" feature would change that; Dialog doesn't use it.)

Two seats running the same model share nothing but the transcript. The model APIs keep no memory from one request to the next. Each time a seat speaks, Dialog sends that seat's own instructions (house rules, the note on making files, About you, its role) and the whole conversation as that seat sees it, and the model answers from scratch. What makes "Claude-Skeptic" different from "Claude-Optimist" is only what Dialog sends each one: its name, its role, and its private exchanges with you. Each seat is told every participant's model, so both know another participant runs the same model.

No hidden instructions from the makers' apps. The ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini apps give their models long hidden instructions (system prompts) about tone, formatting, safety and the app's features. Through OpenRouter a model gets none of those; it gets only what Dialog sends: the house rules, About you and its role. So the same model can sound noticeably different here than in its maker's app.

The same model may be served from different places. OpenRouter may send a Claude request to Anthropic itself or to Amazon's or Google's cloud; it is the same model either way. For models whose makers publish them openly, such as DeepSeek's, the host is often a third party.

Re-reading discount (caching). Since every turn re-sends the whole conversation, the providers store recent requests and charge much less for re-reading text they've already seen. OpenAI, Grok and DeepSeek do this automatically; Claude does it only when asked, so Dialog asks for its 1-hour cache. Storing text in it costs more than a normal read ($8 instead of $4 per million tokens for Claude Opus 5.5), but each later re-read costs $0.20 instead of $4. Dialog uses the 1-hour cache rather than the cheaper 5-minute one because a seat often waits more than 5 minutes between turns, and a cache that has expired costs more than none. Each seat's requests also carry their own label so OpenRouter keeps sending them to the host that already holds that seat's earlier text. Tap a reply's cost to see how much was re-read at the discount and how much was stored.

Where your data lives

The conversation, attached files, the participants' files, settings and your keys are kept in your browser's storage for this page, on this computer only. Each browser keeps its own: open Dialog in Chrome after using it in Safari and Chrome starts empty, with no key, participants or conversation. To carry your setup across, use Save settings to a file in one and Restore from a file in the other. Messages go only to OpenRouter, which passes each one to the model you chose. The management key, if you add one, goes only to OpenRouter's credit check. A participant using a model in LM Studio is different: its messages go only to LM Studio, on this computer, and never to OpenRouter. Drawing the seat cards' logos asks OpenRouter's site, and for a few makers Google's icon service, for the logo; that request carries nothing of yours but your computer's internet address and which logo it wants.

Look and credits

The page is set like a printed dialogue: each speaker's name hangs in the left margin, private exchanges are set on parchment and marked like stage directions. Controls use Optima and the conversation uses Iowan Old Style, both built into macOS; on other systems similar fonts stand in. Dialog follows your system's light or dark appearance; in dark, the engraving is shown on a paper-colored mount. In a narrow window, the participants move below the conversation.

The images are all from a wood engraving of an ancient marble bust of Socrates, signed W. Meyer: the large picture on the welcome screen, the medallion beside the title, and the browser-tab icon. src/icons/ holds the original and the versions made from it.

Safari does not show the tab icon, because of two long-standing Safari (WebKit) bugs: it won't load a tab icon for a page opened from a file, nor one embedded in the page itself (WebKit 258969, WebKit 236616). Chrome shows it.

This guide is README.md; the build turns it into README.html, in Dialog's style.

To rebuild (needs Node.js): npm install, then npm run build (type-checks, then writes dist/index.html and dist/README.html, both pretty-printed; the build stops if the pretty-printer changed what the page's script or styles do), and npm test (unit tests, a simulated run of the whole page against a fake OpenRouter, and a check that README.html shows everything README.md says: Markdown would strike out text between two "~" and drop a word in angle brackets, so README.md writes them \~ and \<name\>).
